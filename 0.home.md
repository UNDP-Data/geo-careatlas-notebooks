Care Atlas

Mapping care to build resilient, gender-responsive societies

Care Atlas brings together analyses built with UNDP's Care Georeferencing Tool (CGT). Each one is an interactive map that shows where people need care, which services exist and who can reach them, to help decide where to invest first. Analyses open in the browser, with maps and filters, and each one documents its data and methods.

About the Care Georeferencing Tool

Demand for childcare, long-term care and disability support is growing as populations age and more women enter the labour market. Climate-related shocks add pressure on households that already do most of the care, much of it unpaid and done by women. Most governments still lack territorial data on where care needs are concentrated, how services are distributed and where the gaps are.

The CGT is an adaptable, low-cost geospatial analytics solution that helps governments visualize and quantify care needs, service availability, accessibility gaps and investment priorities at national and subnational levels. By combining administrative records with alternative data sources (such as web scraping, collaborative field mapping and satellite imagery) the CGT turns scattered, incomplete information into spatial intelligence for decision-making.

What the tool does:

· Maps and consolidates the care service ecosystem. It maps the full ecosystem of services for three core groups — young children, older persons and persons with disabilities — consolidating public, private and community-based centers.

· Estimates the spatial distribution of care needs. Using census data, alongside high-resolution population projections that combine satellite imagery and deep learning, it estimates where people who may require care reside, applying an intersectional lens that captures how gender, poverty, informality, migration status and geographic isolation shape differentiated needs.

· Analyzes accessibility and identifies care deserts. Routing algorithms and spatial analysis determine whether services are effectively accessible, considering travel time, mobility, topography and public transport, revealing areas where demand is high, but care is unreachable, and where services formally exist but remain practically out of reach.

The tool can also overlay climate and hazard data, so risk-management plans can account for the people who depend on care.

Since 2020 the CGT has been applied at local and national level in twelve countries across Latin America and the Caribbean, Africa and Asia and the Pacific. Some partners host their care map on their own servers and connect it to their information systems. Care Atlas gives partners another way in: UNDP hosts the analysis, so a municipality or ministry can start using the results and bring them into policy discussions without setting up its own IT infrastructure.

How to use Care Atlas

· Launch opens an analysis as an interactive application. You do not need an account or to read any code.

· Edit lets signed-in team members update and publish analyses.

· Each analysis states its data sources, date and method, and its main limitations.
